Unlike many people in my field, my background is pretty far from academia. I grew up in a small, relatively poor family, and went to a decent public high school in Louisville, Kentucky. I deeply love my family, and they and my high school are the reason that I remain an artist as well as a physicist (check out my art on this site if I've gotten to updating it!), but this upbringing paled in comparison to the preparation of my peers at Princeton, where I learned about Olympiads by meeting medalists. Early on in my undergrad education, I had many experiences of TAs and peers being downright confused by how much I didn't know when I asked for help. I then decided to work on my own to review the things I didn't know so that I'd be up to speed when I go to ask for help. Little did I know that that's a great strategy for getting C's, and you can spend an entire undergrad doing this. Lesson learned. 

Why am I talking about this? The website of a string theorist tends to be their research, a CV, and maybe a summary of research interests. Why include this? As (to my knowledge) the only black nonbinary theoretical physicist in the world, I first and foremost see no reason why I should follow the exact path of a field full of people who do not share my background. Society itself is surely a chaotic system in some sense, and vastly different initial conditions can still end up at the same place, even if they take a meandering path to get there. Another reason is that alongside physics and math, I have always loved sociology, namely in the behaviors, backgrounds, an paths of marginalized people, so part of this is to turn myself into a sort of case study. Can a random kid from Kentucky become a string theorist without looking like or having the upbringing of every other string theorist? Sometimes you've got to find out by doing it!

Finally, and most importantly, there are so few marginalized people in theoretical physics because of a massive lack of examples to show that we can, in fact, do this. Without examples, there's no suppport. I'd like to serve as the first example of someone like me, and to make the following bet: I believe I am the only black queer string theorist in the world. If the reader can prove me wrong, I owe them a drink.
